# PetroBowl Scoreboard — Design Context

## Design Context

### Users

**Scoreboard (primary):** The live scoreboard is projected on a large LED backdrop screen on stage at the PetroBowl Regional Qualifier in Buenos Aires. Viewed by the entire audience (hundreds of attendees) and the contestants on stage. Also accessible to any spectator on a personal device via URL. Distance from screen means everything must be readable from 20+ meters.

**Participants (secondary):** Petroleum engineering students and professionals at the event, using the prospecting (parimutuel odds) feature on their phones during the competition.

**Admin/Timekeeper (tertiary):** Event organizers only — no one else sees this screen. Design quality is not a priority here.

### Brand Personality

Three words: **high-stakes, prestigious, tense**

The scoreboard should make the audience feel:
- That a lot is on the line — this moment matters
- That the production is top-tier — the industry put its best foot forward
- That it is an honor to participate in this event
- That they are watching something worthy of the profession's best talent

### Aesthetic Direction

**Reference:** UEFA Champions League ceremony + DOTA 2 The International arena visuals.

What these share:
- Dark, near-black backgrounds with dramatic depth — not flat black, but layered with subtle atmospheric gradients
- Gold as the metal of achievement — used sparingly, never diluted, earned by performance
- Massive score/stat typography that commands the space
- The sense of two forces about to collide — confrontational layout geometry
- Ceremonial transitions: state changes feel like events, not just data updates
- Premium restraint: nothing is garish, everything is intentional

**Anti-references:** Generic sports scoreboards (scorebug style), startup SaaS dashboards, anything that reads as "a developer made a dark-mode app." The scoreboard should look like it was designed for an arena, not an office.

**Theme:** Dark always. This is a stage event with dramatic lighting. The screen must pop against a dark auditorium.

**Color palette (from SPE brand — must be respected):**
- Deep navy `#003B71` — structural surfaces, deep backgrounds
- Accent blue `#0072CE` — highlights, active states
- Gold `#F2A900` — leading scores, achievements, streaks, CTAs — never overused
- Near-black `#1A1A2E` — primary background canvas
- Correct green `#00C853` — correct answer indicators
- Incorrect red `#FF1744` — incorrect answer indicators
- White `#FFFFFF` — primary text

**Typography direction:** The display font should feel industrial-athletic and condensed — the kind of letterform you'd see on a stadium signage or a championship trophy plaque. Pair it with a body font that's clean and technical without feeling like a startup. Existing brand fonts are Myriad Variable Concept (titles) + Stolzl (body); treat these as the target feel when selecting Google Fonts fallbacks.

### Design Principles

1. **Cinematic over functional** — Every screen should feel like it belongs in a broadcast production. If it could appear in a TV cutaway shot, it's good enough.

2. **High-contrast, distance-readable clarity** — At LED scale, the audience reads from 20 meters away. Scores, team names, and match state must be instantly legible at any size. Bold, large, unambiguous — never rely on subtle detail to communicate important state.

3. **Gold earns its place** — Gold is the metal of winning. Reserve it for the leading score, streaks, wins, and moments of triumph. The moment gold appears everywhere, it means nothing. Scarcity is what makes it feel earned.

4. **Tension architecture** — The scoreboard layout should make you feel the confrontation. The two teams should feel like opposing forces separated by the score, not just two columns of data. Asymmetric emphasis when one team leads.

5. **Moments, not just state changes** — Score updates, match starts, and finishes should feel like events. Motion should be purposeful and impactful — not decorative, but cinematic. A correct answer should feel like a point scored in a stadium.

### Priority Order

1. **Scoreboard (`/`)** — the hero; highest design priority
2. **Participant app (`/p/*`)** — spectators on phones; secondary priority
3. **Admin/Timekeeper (`/timekeeper/*`)** — organizers only; low priority
